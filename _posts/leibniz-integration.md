Step 1: Finite LocalizationBecause the path \(\gamma \) is compact, we cover it with a finite number of coordinate charts. This allows us to focus entirely on a single segment of the curve contained within a single local chart \(\phi: U \to \mathbb{R}^d\).Step 2: Freezing Velocity and Scaling SpeedLet \(P = \{t_0, t_1, \dots, t_n\}\) be a finite partition of the parameter space. For any subinterval \([t_{i-1}, t_i]\) with width \(\Delta t_i = t_i - t_{i-1}\), we define the velocity vector at the start of the interval:\(v_{i-1}=\.{\gamma }(t_{i-1})\in T_{\gamma (t_{i-1})}M\)We construct a virtual straight-line germ \(\sigma_i(s)\) inside the flat space \(\mathbb{R}^{d}\), parameterized by a local variable \(s \in [0, 1]\):\(\sigma _{i}(s)=\phi ^{-1}\left(\phi (\gamma (t_{i-1}))+s\cdot \Delta t_{i}\cdot d\phi (v_{i-1})\right)\)By multiplying the velocity by \(\Delta t_i\), the virtual curve germ is forced to travel at an incredibly slow speed proportional to the partition width.Step 3: Resolving the Local Inner LimitWe feed this slow virtual germ into the co-germ 1-form \(\omega \). By the rescaling law of co-germs, the form naturally extracts the \(\Delta t_i\) speed factor during its internal pointwise limit process:\(\omega _{\gamma (t_{i-1})}\left([\sigma _{i}]_{0}\right)=\lim _{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\dots }{s}=\Delta t_{i}\cdot \omega _{\gamma (t_{i-1})}\left([\text{unit-speed\ version}]\right)\)Step 4: Resolving the Global Outer LimitAfter the inner limit resolves itself into a concrete real number for every segment, we assemble the finite sum \(S_{P}\):\(S_{P}=\sum _{i=1}^{n}\omega _{\gamma (t_{i-1})}\left([\sigma _{i}]_{0}\right)\)The final integral is defined as the net limit over the directed set of all possible finite partition refinements:\(\int _{\gamma }\omega =\lim _{\text{mesh}\rightarrow 0}\sum _{i=1}^{n}\omega _{\gamma (t_{i-1})}\left([\sigma _{i}]_{0}\right)\)🧠 Memory Anchors for Future ReviewWhy does the sum stay finite? Refining a partition does not mean appending new stuff; it means subdividing an existing segment. A 1-form is strictly linear, so splitting a vector in half replaces one old term with two new terms that each have exactly half the value.Do I multiply or divide? A 1-form like \(dx\) has the dimension \(T^{-1}\) because it wants to eat a velocity. To turn it into a pure number, you must multiply it by a step size \(\Delta t\) (\(T\)). You never divide.The Traffic Jam of Limits: The two limits are strictly nested. The inner limit (\(\epsilon \to 0\)) happens point-by-point inside the frozen germ to define the form's value. The outer limit (\(\Delta t \to 0\)) happens globally afterward to glue the partition pieces together.
